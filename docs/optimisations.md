# PSP2DC: Optimisations for Later

Things that would make PSP2DC faster, lighter or easier to use, but that nothing needs now. Each says what it would gain, what it would cost, and what is known. They are added as they are found and done only when chosen.

| # | What | Gain | Cost | Known |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pictures kept on the Memory Stick** (companion, phase 3's rest; [companion E.8](psp2dc-companion.md)) | A game's sprites cross the cable once ever, not at every start or replug: 300 KB of pictures is about 5 s each time now | The library asks first by the picture's checksum; the PSP saves what it was sent and loads what it has. PSP application, library, one more record each way | Designed, not built. The risk is a wrong picture from a stale or damaged file: each file carries the key and a checksum of its own, and a picture is copied whole before it is saved |
| 2 | **Files written faster** (companion, phase 4) | Writes measured 38 KB a second on a Dreamcast against 54 for reads and 55 in the simulator | Unknown until measured | Cause not known. One guess: the Memory Stick taking the bytes 990 at a time (one `sceIoWrite` per piece); a buffer of several pieces would test it |
| 3 | **More than one companion command a vblank** | The Dreamcast to PSP path is one command a vblank today, about 60 KB a second at most; two would double it | Library only, if KallistiOS sends a second frame of the library's own in the same vblank | Possible in KallistiOS's queue by its code, not tried on hardware ([companion A.2](psp2dc-companion.md)) |
| 4 | **A larger read window** (companion, phase 4) | Reads are at 54 KB a second with the 3 KB window, near the 60 KB ceiling of one command a vblank; more only with 3 | The adapter's 4 KB queue towards the Dreamcast made larger, so the window can grow: firmware | Worth it only after 3 |
| 5 | **Controller options, if games need them** | The mapping is one to one and fixed (main.c, `map_input`): a remapping screen would only move buttons around. Two gaps are real: the nub is small and imprecise, and L and R send a trigger fully pressed or not at all | One option each, only for a game that needs it: the d-pad as the stick; L and R as a part-pressed trigger (held longer, pressed further, or similar) | Waits on game testing to show a game that needs either. Was "configurable button mapping" in the plans |
| 6 | **The PSP's buttons sampled faster** | About half of what PSP2DC adds to a press (8 ms of about 10 on average, [design 5.1a](psp2dc.md)) is waiting for the PSP's once-a-frame sample | The PSP's controller sampled on a timer instead of at the display's refresh, and INPUT sent when the buttons change, apart from the main loop | Not checked how fast the PSP can sample, or whether the adapter then passes it on sooner. Worth it only if a game feels late |
| 7 | **Scenes the PSP keeps: retained vector graphics** (companion; for any game, not one kind of map) | A map, a radar or a track is sent once; after that only what changes crosses the cable: a camera (position, zoom, rotation), an object's position, a line shown or hidden, a few bytes each. The PSP transforms, clips and draws, so it can pan, zoom and turn at its own 60 frames a second whatever the link carries. Doom 64's map costs about 2.8 KB a redraw today as lines (200 of them), against 88 KB as a bitmap | A new protocol level, kept beside the drawing records there are. Two kinds of record: geometry (lines, layers, objects: at first and when it changes) and state (the camera, an object's position and angle, what is visible). PSP application: both kept, then transform, clip, draw. Library: calls to make a scene in a region, add and remove lines, show and hide them, place objects, set the camera; all of it sent again at a new session, as widgets are. To settle in the design: how much geometry the PSP keeps, world coordinates and their precision, ids for lines, objects and layers, how a scene is kept in step when records are lost | Not designed. The program stays the one that says what is visible: a level sent whole must not show rooms not yet seen. Measured with the lines as they are, Doom 64 on a Dreamcast: the game keeps 60 frames a second and spends 0.3 to 0.4 ms a frame on the PSP (under 1 ms at worst); moving, the map is drawn 15 to 22 times a second from 130 to 155 lines, 30 to 42 KB a second, and 30 to 42 commands a second are answered of the 60 sent for (none unanswered). So the cable is not the limit now: see 9 first |
| 8 | **A region on the theme's background** (companion) | A picture in a region sits on the screen's colour in every theme. Today a region is a rectangle of the program's own pixels and the program is not told the theme: Doom 64's face is drawn on the first theme's background and shows as a box in the others | Either the PSP says its colours in CAPS (and again when the theme changes), or a palette entry can mean "the screen behind". PSP application and library; a new level | Not designed. The second way needs nothing sent when the theme changes |
| 9 | **The PSP takes the adapter's data apart from its drawing** (companion) | Doom 64's map at about 30 drawings a second in place of 15 to 22, with nothing changed in the protocol or in the game: a command every frame, as in the simulator (53 KB a second there) | PSP application: the exchange with the adapter no longer once a drawn frame (a thread of its own, or more than one exchange a frame), or the companion's screen drawn in less than a frame | The cause is inferred, not measured: the PSP application waits for the display once a frame and exchanges once a frame; 30 commands a second is what a PSP drawing 30 frames a second would take, 42 a mix of 30 and 60. The adapter's count of "again" answers (Settings > Details on the PSP) rising while the map moves would confirm it; the PSP's own frame time is not yet shown anywhere |
